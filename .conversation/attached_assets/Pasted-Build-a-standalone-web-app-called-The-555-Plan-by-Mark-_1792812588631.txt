Build a standalone web app called "The 555 Plan" by Mark Harbert. It costs users nothing. There are no payments, no checkout, and no paid tiers anywhere in the app. Users must register for an account to use it. The app helps beginners get over their fear of being on camera and record their first videos, step by step, right inside the browser.

**Two big goals for the whole build:**
1. **It must feel premium.** It costs nothing, but it should never feel cheap. Users should feel like this is the most incredible no-cost app they've ever been given, and like something they want to come back to over and over.
2. **It must lead them step by step.** Every screen should make the next step obvious: do this, then do this, straight into the 555 Plan.

**Copy rule: never use the word "free" anywhere in the user-facing app.** Not on the landing page, buttons, emails, or anywhere else. Use phrases like "no-cost account," "on the house," or "yours to keep" when it needs saying at all. (The word may appear in this prompt only as an instruction to you.)

**Brand guide:** I am attaching my brand guide to this prompt. Build the entire app from it: the colors, fonts, logo usage, spacing, button styles, and layout guidance. Where the brand guide and this prompt differ on a visual detail, follow the brand guide.

Mark teaches that you can't make your 100th video without making your first. The app walks people through 15 small video "missions" in three rounds of five, records each one in the browser, tracks how their confidence grows, and then graduates them to their first public video.

Build it in the phases listed at the bottom. Finish and test each phase before starting the next.

## 1. Tech and general requirements

- Full-stack web app with a Postgres database.
- Fully responsive. It must work well on desktop and laptop (the main use case, with webcam recording) and still be usable on phones and tablets.
- User accounts (name, email, password) so users can come back and keep their progress. Include password reset by email.
- Two roles: `user` and `admin`. Admins get a full backend (section 13).
- All AI calls happen on the server. Never expose API keys to the browser.
- Store all app copy (mission prompts, mindset cards, default cue cards) in the database or a single content file so it can be edited later without code changes.

**ClickFunnels integration (direct API, not a webhook)**
When someone registers, add them to Mark's ClickFunnels workspace as a contact using the ClickFunnels v2 API.

- Read the current API docs at https://developers.myclickfunnels.com before writing this code and follow them exactly.
- Authenticate with a Bearer token. Store everything in environment variables (Replit Secrets), never in code:
  - `CLICKFUNNELS_API_TOKEN`
  - `CLICKFUNNELS_SUBDOMAIN` (the account subdomain, so the API base is `https://{subdomain}.myclickfunnels.com/api/v2`)
  - `CLICKFUNNELS_WORKSPACE_ID`
- On registration, upsert the contact in the workspace (create it, or update it if the email already exists in ClickFunnels) with first name, last name, and email. Save the returned ClickFunnels contact ID on the user record.
- Apply a tag `555 Plan - Registered`. Create the tag in the workspace if it doesn't exist yet.
- Apply progress tags as the user moves through the app, so Mark can trigger ClickFunnels workflows and emails:
  - `555 Plan - Onboarding Done`
  - `555 Plan - Round 1 Done`
  - `555 Plan - Round 2 Done`
  - `555 Plan - Round 3 Done`
  - `555 Plan - Graduated`
  - `555 Plan - 30 Day Challenge Started`
  - `555 Plan - 30 Day Challenge Done`
- Let the admin rename these tags in Settings (section 13) without code changes.
- Run the ClickFunnels calls in the background. A ClickFunnels error must never block registration or progress. If a call fails, save it to a `sync_queue` table and retry with backoff (for example after 1 minute, 5 minutes, 30 minutes, 2 hours). Store the last error message.
- Track sync status on each user: synced, pending, or failed.
- Add a "Test ClickFunnels Connection" button in admin Settings that confirms the token, subdomain, and workspace work.

- Being vulnerable
- Not knowing what to say
- Negative comments and trolls

**Step 2: About You.** These power the AI cue cards.

- Who do you want to reach? (open text)

**Step 3: My Plan.** Turn these questions into a simple form and show the result as "My Video Plan" on their dashboard:

- When are my pockets of time? (morning, lunch break, evening, weekends; multi-select)
- What can I delegate or drop to make more time? (open text)
- Which notifications will I turn off while I record? (open text)
- Who will be my accountability partner? (name, optional)

Add a line under the form: "The best plan is the one you'll actually follow."

## 5. Mindset Cards

Short reframes, one per fear. After the Fear Check, show the cards that match the fears they picked, one at a time, swipeable or with a "Next" button. Each card is a headline plus 2 to 4 short sentences. Keep all of them available later in a "Mindset" page.

Seed these cards (edit wording lightly if needed, keep the meaning and tone):

1. **It's about them, not you.** Every viewer speaks one language: "What's in it for me?" When you focus on helping the person watching, the fear gets quieter.
2. **Not everyone will like you. Good.** Good marketing attracts the right people and repels the rest. If 100 people watch and 2 respond, your video did its job.
3. **Vulnerable is relatable.** Share real challenges you've faced. People connect with what they can relate to. Use good judgment on what stays private.
4. **You already know what to say.** You could talk about your passion for hours with a friend. The red light on the camera doesn't change what you know.
5. **Trolls are about them.** People who attack you usually aren't at peace with themselves. Have compassion, then block and delete. Blocking and deleting is a money-making activity.
6. **Lock in who you are.** When you know your identity, comments can't shake it. Watch your "I am" and "I always" statements. What you focus on grows.
7. **Perfectionism is a cover for insecurity.** Imperfect action beats perfect inaction every single day. You can't steer a parked car.
8. **Embrace the suck.** Your first video will be bad. So was everyone's. Jump out of the plane and figure out the parachute on the way down.
9. **You don't need to be techie.** If you can post a photo, you can post a video. Same button.

## 6. The 555 Plan: 15 Missions

Three rounds of five missions. Missions unlock in order. Round 2 unlocks when all of Round 1 is done. Round 3 unlocks when Round 2 is done.

### Round 1: Just Me
Intro text: "These five videos are for your eyes only. Nobody else sees them. Keep each one to 1 minute or less. The goal is simple: get comfortable on camera."
Max length: 60 seconds.

1. **Share your why.** The passion and reasons driving your business.
2. **A peek into your day.** A snapshot of your daily life with a personal touch.
3. **A book that shaped you.** A book that changed your life or business.
4. **A hobby that sparks you.** Something that recharges and inspires you.
5. **A challenge you overcame.** A personal challenge and how you got through it.

### Round 2: Test Group
Intro text: "Now you'll post five 1-minute videos in a private Facebook group where you are the only member. Still private, but you're building the habit of hitting post."
Max length: 60 seconds.
Include a collapsible "How to create your private Facebook test group" help box with simple steps: on Facebook go to Groups, click Create New Group, name it something like "My Video Practice," set privacy to Private, skip inviting anyone, and create it. Add a note that Facebook changes its menus often, so if a step looks different, search YouTube for "how to create a Facebook group."

1. **Your biggest business lesson.**
2. **A mentor who made a difference.**
3. **Advice to your earlier self.** What you wish you knew when you started.
4. **Your go-to productivity tip.**
5. **Something you're learning right now.** (Show an alternate option the user can switch to: "Why building an email list matters," for internet marketers.)

Each Round 2 mission adds a checkbox: "I posted this in my test group."

### Round 3: Going Live
Intro text: "Five short live videos, 1 to 2 minutes each, in your private test group. Do a practice take here first if you want, then go live on Facebook."
Max length: 120 seconds.

1. **Your morning routine.** How you start your day.
2. **Work-life harmony.** How you balance it all (or how you're trying to).
3. **Your favorite quote.** A quote that guides you and why.
4. **Learning on the go.** A new skill you're picking up in your business.
5. **Your values in business.** How your personal values or faith shape your decisions.

Each Round 3 mission adds a checkbox: "I went live in my test group."

### Mission screen layout (desktop)
- Left side: the in-browser recorder (see section 7).
- Right side: the mission title, a one-line prompt, and the **cue cards**: 3 or 4 short bullet points to talk from. These are bullets, not a script. Add a small note: "Glance, don't read. Talk like you're telling a friend."
- Under the recorder: "Mark as Done" (red button). When clicked, ask "How did that feel?" with a 1 to 10 slider, save it, then show a short encouraging message and a "Next Mission" button.
- On mobile, stack the cue cards above the recorder.

A mission can be marked done without recording in the app (some people will record on their phone). Recording is encouraged, not required.

## 7. In-Browser Video Recorder (core feature)

Users must be able to turn on their webcam and record each mission right inside the app.

**Camera setup**
- Use `navigator.mediaDevices.getUserMedia` for video and audio.
- Show a live preview, mirrored (like a selfie camera). The saved recording is not mirrored.
- Dropdowns to choose camera and microphone (`enumerateDevices`). Remember their choice.
- A small live audio level meter so they can see the mic is working.
- Clear, friendly messages when permission is denied, no camera is found, or the camera is in use by another app, with steps to fix it in Chrome, Edge, Safari, and Firefox.
- Turn the camera off when they leave the page or finish recording.

**Recording**
- Use `MediaRecorder`. Pick a supported format with `MediaRecorder.isTypeSupported`: prefer `video/mp4` when supported (Safari), otherwise `video/webm;codecs=vp9,opus`, then `video/webm`.
- Big red "Start Recording" button, then a 3, 2, 1 countdown on screen.
- While recording: a visible timer counting up, a red recording dot, and a "Stop" button.
- Max length per round (60 seconds for Rounds 1 and 2, 120 seconds for Round 3). At 10 seconds left, show a gentle "Wrap it up" notice. At the limit, stop automatically.
- The cue cards stay visible next to the camera while recording.

**After recording**
- Show a playback player of the take.
- Buttons: "Keep This Take," "Record Again," "Download," "Delete."
- Users can save multiple takes per mission and pick one as their favorite.
- Download saves the file to their computer with a clear name like `555-Round1-Mission1-Share-Your-Why.webm` (use .mp4 if recorded in mp4).

**Storage (important)**
- Store videos privately in the user's own browser using IndexedDB. Do not upload videos to the server. This keeps practice videos 100 percent private (Round 1 is "for your eyes only") and keeps hosting costs at zero.
- Only save metadata on the server: mission ID, take number, duration in seconds, date recorded, favorite flag, and whether it was downloaded or deleted. Never the video itself. This metadata powers the admin stats in section 13, so record it every time a take is saved, downloaded, or deleted.
- Show a clear note on the "My Videos" page: "Your videos are saved privately in this browser on this computer. They don't sync to other devices. Download any video you want to keep."
- Request persistent storage with `navigator.storage.persist()` and show how much space is used (`navigator.storage.estimate()`).
- If a user clears their browser data, the videos are gone, so prompt them to download favorites after each round.

**My Videos page**
- A grid of all their saved takes grouped by round and mission, with a thumbnail, duration, and date.
- Play, download, and delete each one. Confirm before deleting.
- "Download all from this round" button that downloads them one by one.

## 8. AI-Personalized Cue Cards

Each mission has default cue cards (written below), and the app also generates personalized ones from the user's profile.

- When a user opens a mission for the first time, call an AI model on the server with: the mission title and prompt, the user's answers from "About You" (what they do, who they reach, why they started), and the instructions below. Save the result in the database so it is generated only once per user per mission.
- Show the personalized cue cards by default, with a small toggle to see the default ones.
- A "Give me different ideas" button regenerates them, limited to 3 regenerations per mission per user.
- Rate limit AI calls per user per day (for example 40) to control cost. If the AI call fails or the limit is hit, show the default cue cards and never block the mission.

AI instructions for cue cards:
- Return 3 or 4 bullets as JSON.
- Each bullet is a talking point starter, 12 words or fewer.
- Bullets, not a script. No full sentences to read word for word.
- Specific to the user's business and audience when it fits the mission. Personal missions (book, hobby, morning routine) should stay personal and only lightly connect to their business.
- Warm, encouraging, simple language. No hype, no jargon, no em dashes.

Default cue cards (fallback and starting point; write 3 or 4 short bullets for each of the 15 missions in this same style). Example for "Share your why":
- The moment you decided to start
- Who you're doing this for
- What life looks like when it works
- One word that sums up your why

## 9. Graduation: First Public Video Builder

Unlocks after all 15 missions. Celebrate it with a big congratulations screen showing their baseline comfort score next to their latest score.

Then a guided builder for their first public video using Mark's 5-part long video formula. One section per step, each with a short explanation and a text box:

1. **The Hook.** You have 1 to 3 seconds. Tell them what you're going to share. Mark's rule: master the hook.
2. **Set the Hook.** Drive it home. Describe the problem they're feeling so they think "you're talking to me."
3. **First Call to Action (optional).** Who you are and why they should subscribe or follow.
4. **High-Value Content.** Deliver on the promise. Suggest a list format ("3 ways to...", "5 mistakes...") with fields for each point.
5. **Final Call to Action.** Tell them exactly what to do next, like "Type INFO below and I'll reach out."

Features:
- "Help me with my hook" button: AI suggests 3 hook options from their topic and profile (counts toward the daily AI limit).
- A **Hook Templates** library: a searchable list of fill-in-the-blank hooks. Seed it with 20 sample templates. Admin can import more from a CSV (one template per row, optional category column). Clicking a template drops it into the Hook box.
- Show the finished outline as a clean bullet "notepad" they can keep next to the recorder.
- Then open the recorder for this video (max 10 minutes), with the outline shown as cue cards.
